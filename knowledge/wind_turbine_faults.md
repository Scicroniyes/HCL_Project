## Gearbox overheating
Symptoms: gearbox oil temperature high, gearbox bearing temperature high, often during high load. Vibration may stay normal in the early stage.
Probable causes: low gearbox oil level or degraded oil; blocked oil cooler or failed cooling fan or pump; excessive friction from worn gears or bearings; sustained operation at full load in high ambient temperature.
Recommended checks: check the oil level and oil condition (colour, contamination); inspect the oil cooler, cooling fan and oil pump; take an oil sample and look for metal particles; compare the temperature with the load and ambient temperature to see if it is abnormal for the conditions.
Risk: continued operation can cause bearing and gear damage and an expensive gearbox failure. Reduce load or stop the turbine if the temperature keeps rising.

## Gearbox bearing wear
Symptoms: vibration rising over time, gearbox bearing temperature slightly above normal, sometimes a metallic noise.
Probable causes: worn or pitted bearing; insufficient or contaminated lubrication; shaft misalignment; overloading.
Recommended checks: run a vibration analysis on the drivetrain; inspect lubrication and oil filter condition; check shaft alignment; inspect for metal particles in the oil.
Risk: bearing wear develops gradually and can end in bearing seizure, so early detection allows planned repair.

## Generator overheating
Symptoms: generator bearing temperature high, stator winding temperature high, power output may drop.
Probable causes: blocked generator cooling air path or dirty filters; failed generator cooling fan; electrical overload; winding insulation deterioration; high ambient temperature.
Recommended checks: inspect the generator cooling fan and air filters; check stator winding insulation resistance; review recent power output for overload; check the electrical connections for loose or heated terminals.
Risk: overheated windings lose insulation life quickly, and a winding short can cause a long outage.

## Excessive vibration
Symptoms: vibration above normal, possibly together with reduced power or irregular rotor speed.
Probable causes: blade imbalance caused by ice, dirt, damage or pitch error; loose mounting or tower bolts; early-stage bearing or gear wear; rotor or shaft misalignment.
Recommended checks: inspect the blades for ice, damage or dirt; check bolt tightness on the nacelle and tower; compare vibration at different rotor speeds; check the pitch angles of all three blades.
Risk: continued vibration loads the whole drivetrain and tower, and can crack components. Stop the turbine if vibration reaches the critical level.

## Yaw misalignment
Symptoms: active power lower than expected for the wind speed while all temperatures and vibration look normal.
Probable causes: nacelle not facing the wind because of a faulty wind direction sensor; yaw drive or brake problem; yaw control calibration error.
Recommended checks: compare the wind direction sensor with a nearby reference; inspect the yaw drive and brake; verify the yaw calibration; compare the power curve with the expected curve.
Risk: no immediate damage, but energy production is lost every day until it is fixed.
